29 · HOME & ASK

Ask anything. Hear this month. Help with one tap.

Shaped like a chat app's opening screen. A big headline sits in the middle with four buttons under it: Continue, Daily learning, This month's recordings, Help check. The one box is docked at the bottom on a phone, under the thumb, and in the centre under the headline on a desktop. Scroll down and this month's recordings open by day: one featured card in ink with its paragraph map (each bar is a paragraph, amber until checked, green once checked), then the rest as quiet rows. A recording with no transcript yet offers “Ask for a transcript”, which asks the machine.

The one box

Names and dates, at onceAs you type, the box finds sefarim, sichos and farbrengens. It already reads dates, parshiyos and chagim (“ליל י״ג תשרי תשמ״ח”, “בראשית תשמ״ז”). Nothing new is needed for this part.
A question gets an answer from the libraryWhen the words are a question, Ask finds the places by meaning (search by idea, which exists today) and writes a short answer built only from them. Every sentence carries its source: a sicha, a page, or a moment in a recording.
Always labelled, never inventedThe answer carries the amber “written by a machine” mark. When the library has no source, it says so and shows what it did find. It never answers from outside the library, and never puts words in the Rebbe's mouth: quotes are copied word for word from the source, with a link.
The sources come firstOn a phone, the sources sit right under the answer as rows you can tap, each opening at its line or moment. “Open in search” leads to the full search page, with its filters.

What makes someone press play

The page has two goals: press play on a recording with its words, or search. Status (machine, checked, how many are waiting) answers a helper's question, not a listener's, so it comes off the home page. It stays where it belongs: the label on the transcript itself, and the Help level. What makes someone press play is what the farbrengen is about, and why it matters today. The catalog already holds both.

Why today: the same kviusThe cards come from the year of farbrengens whose calendar falls like this one (lib/week.ts kviusYears). For 5787 that is תשל״ו, so this Shabbos Bereishis is the Shabbos Bereishis of תשל״ו, which itself speaks of “קביעות שנה זו”. A card leads to reading; it gets ▶ when the catalog has the recording. Under them, “Listen with the words” keeps the recordings with a transcript from these days.
What it is aboutTwo or three lines from the farbrengen's own table of contents (the catalog holds it for each one, credited to mafteiach.app), and its maamar if it has one, such as “ד״ה בורא ניב שפתיים”. These are real words from the index, not a machine's summary.
One tap to sound and words▶ starts it in the site's player. Listen with the text opens the transcript page already playing, with the words lit as they are said. A small line says what else it has: audio, transcript, video, written hanacha.
Topics lead to searchThe topics of these days become chips (הקהל, בראשית ברא, מלכתחילה אריבער). One tap fills the box and asks, so the two goals feed each other.

Then the day, kept small

“Continue where you stopped” (reading or listening, shared between your phone and computer), then today's learning as one row of chips that tick off as you learn: Chumash, Tehillim, Tanya, Rambam, Hayom Yom. A farbrengen held on this day in the Rebbe's years comes last. All of this exists today and keeps its data; only the order and the look change.

What it needs that is new

One API call, /v1/ask. It runs the search by meaning, then a model writes the answer from the passages it found and returns the answer, the quotes and the sources. It is cached by question, and it is off until the keepers turn it on. Everything else on the page reads what the API already serves: events by month with their transcripts, and what waits to be checked.
