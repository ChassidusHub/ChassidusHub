Simple on the surface. Every power one step away.

A reader who only wants the words sees the words. Someone who helps sees the tools to help. A steward sees everything. It is one site, one look and one set of parts, and each layer opens from the one before it.

Mobile first. Every screen starts at phone size: one column, the answers in reach of the thumb at the bottom of the screen, and 44px targets. A larger screen adds a second column and keyboard shortcuts; it never adds a different way of working.

Six principles

01

The words are the interface

The text is the largest and calmest thing on every page. Chrome shrinks, steps back and comes back when it is needed.

02

One accent, quiet everything else

The red of a title page marks links, focus and the one main action. Paper, ink and rules do the rest.

03

Power in layers, never in clutter

Read, then Help, then Steward. Each page has one ⋯ menu that holds history, versions, scans and editions, so nothing is missing and nothing is in the way.

04

Plain words

The plan's words throughout: Suggestion, Report, Approve, Project, Set. GitHub terms appear only in help.

05

Phone first, Hebrew first

Every screen is designed at 390px first and grows from there. It is right to left first, with English mirrored from the same parts.

06

Machine output is always labelled

Words no person has checked carry a small amber dot. It is quiet, it is never a banner, and it is never left out.

Three levels, one site

One setting, What you see, chooses the level, and anyone can change it in one tap. A higher level only adds things, it never moves what was already there. Signing in or being made a steward suggests the next level up; nothing switches by itself.

How deep settings stay simple

  1. One page, with a search box. Every setting lives on /account, so you type “font” and land on it. There are no settings hidden on other pages.
  2. Grouped by what you are doing: Reading, Listening, Daily learning, Language, Notifications, Helping, Account, Apps & developers.
  3. Every row says what it does, in one line. It shows the current value and changes in place. Changes save at once and say “Saved”; there is no Save button to forget.
  4. The common settings show first. Each group's “More options” opens the rest in place, right under them, not on another page.
  5. A changed row shows a dot and a Reset button, so you can always find your way back to the defaults.
  6. Reading settings preview live on real text, and the same controls open from Aa in the reader.
